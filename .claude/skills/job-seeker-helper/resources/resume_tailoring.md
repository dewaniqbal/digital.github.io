# Resume Tailoring Guide

## Goal

Make the resume more relevant to a specific job while preserving the candidate's truth.

## Inputs

- Resume/CV
- Job description
- Target role and company
- Candidate constraints, such as location, visa/work authorization if relevant, and seniority target

## ATS keyword audit

Group terms into:

- matched keywords: present in both resume and JD
- missing keywords: in JD but not resume
- unsupported keywords: in JD but no candidate evidence
- alternative phrasing: candidate has related experience but uses different language

Do not force every missing keyword into the resume. Only add a keyword when it truthfully describes the candidate's experience.

## Resume summary formula

Use 2 to 4 lines:

```text
[Role identity] with [years/scope if true] in [domain]. Experienced in [top 2-4 truthful skills from JD]. Known for [business outcome or working style]. Seeking to help [company/team] [role-specific outcome].
```

## Bullet rewrite formula

Use:

```text
Action verb + scope/context + tool/method + measurable result or business value.
```

Examples:

- Weak: Responsible for dashboards.
- Better: Built Tableau dashboards for sales and operations teams, helping stakeholders monitor weekly revenue, pipeline, and customer trends.
- Better with true metric: Built Tableau dashboards used by 25+ sales and operations stakeholders to monitor weekly revenue, pipeline, and customer trends.

## Red flags

Do not:

- add fake metrics
- claim tools not used
- inflate seniority
- hide career gaps with false dates
- rewrite unrelated work as direct experience

Use placeholders:

- `[add metric if true]`
- `[confirm tool/version]`
- `[add team size if true]`

## Final resume sections

Recommended order:

1. Name and contact
2. Targeted headline
3. Summary
4. Core skills
5. Experience
6. Projects, if relevant
7. Education and certifications

For students or career switchers, projects may come before experience if they are stronger evidence for the target role.
